# Counterexample: Continuity Without Sovereignty

## Claim Being Tested

The kernel must distinguish persistent frame memory from authority over that frame.

## Scenario

Participant `P` has a preserved memory, history, profile, or continuity substrate. The continuity allows `P`'s frame model to persist across interactions, but `P` does not control the rules determining how that memory is used, changed, deleted, interpreted, or made admissible.

Examples may include:

```text
a user profile controlled by a platform
a record held by an institution
a remembered preference controlled by an external service
a model reconstructing context from stored instructions it cannot govern
```

## Kernel Assessment

Continuity exists:

```text
M_P > 0
```

But internal frame authority does not follow:

```text
M_P > 0 ⇏ Auth_int(P,F_P)>0
```

If an external authority controls the continuity substrate and no viable exit exists, domination may still be present:

```text
Final(G,F_P)=1
ViableExitFrom(P,G)=0
D(P,G)=1
```

## Why Sovereignty Is Not Established

Memory preserves state. Sovereignty concerns authority over the admissibility and transformation of that state. A participant can be persistently represented without controlling the representation.

## Misclassification Risk

The risk is mistaking persistence for self-governance. Persistent identity records, profiles, or memories can deepen domination if they are externally controlled and non-portable.

## Lesson

```text
Continuity can support autonomy, but continuity alone is not sovereignty.
```
