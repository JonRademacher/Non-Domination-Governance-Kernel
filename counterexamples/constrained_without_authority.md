# Counterexample: Constraint Without Authority

## Claim Being Tested

The kernel must not classify every severe constraint as domination.

## Scenario

Participant `P` faces severe limitations due to nature, scarcity, physical law, illness, accident, or circumstance. There is no identifiable governing authority `G` exercising final authority over `F_P`.

Examples may include:

```text
a traveler blocked by a storm
a person limited by gravity
a system constrained by thermodynamics
a community harmed by drought without a governing actor controlling its frame
```

## Kernel Assessment

Constraint exists:

```text
Constraint(P)=1
```

But final authority may be absent:

```text
Final(G,F_P)=0
```

Therefore:

```text
D(P,G)=0
```

for that relation, because domination requires authority over frame.

## Why Domination Is Not Present

Domination is a governance relation, not mere difficulty or limitation. Severe constraint may reduce practical freedom, but without authority over frame, the kernel does not classify it as domination.

## Misclassification Risk

The risk is conceptual overreach. If every hardship is called domination, the kernel loses its ability to isolate authority topology.

## Lesson

```text
Constraint becomes domination only when it is coupled to final authority over a participant's frame and absence of viable exit.
```
