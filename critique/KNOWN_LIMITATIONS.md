# Known Limitations

## 1. The Kernel Is Structural, Not Complete Ethics

The kernel classifies domination topology. It does not answer every moral, legal, political, or design question.

## 2. Frame Detection Remains Contestable

Determining whether a participant has a structural frame may be difficult in borderline cases.

## 3. Exit Viability May Be Contextual

Standing-preserving exit can depend on social, economic, legal, technical, or infrastructural context.

## 4. Repairability Is Not Fully Quantified

The framework distinguishes repairable, closed, and recursive domination, but richer measures of repair effectiveness remain future work.

## 5. The Kernel Does Not Establish Consciousness

Positive domination classification does not imply consciousness, personhood, suffering, legal rights, or moral patienthood.

## 6. Evidence Availability Limits Applied Classification

In many institutional or technical systems, relevant governance evidence may be unavailable. In those cases the correct output is low confidence, not overconfident classification.

## 7. Cross-Domain Translation Requires Care

The theory is portable only if frame, authority, exit, standing, and repair are translated faithfully into the target domain.

## 8. Governance Justification Is Separate

A justified governance system may still instantiate domination topology. The kernel classifies topology; it does not alone settle justification.
